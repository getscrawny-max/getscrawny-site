Adaptive motor coaching for planning, practice, and access.

Apraxia can interrupt the connection between intention and movement. A person may understand the task, want to participate, and still need the movement broken into accessible motor steps.

Sessions are one-on-one and appointment-only. Each appointment is scheduled as a one-hour block with about 45 minutes of active coaching, with room for transition, communication, regulation, and pacing.

This is coaching-based support, not occupational therapy. Work may support motor initiation, sequencing, coordination, balance, strength, endurance, regulation, participation, and independence.

01

Motor planning

Clear setup, modeling, rhythm, and body-based language help make the next action easier to access.

02

Functional movement

Coaching supports useful movement patterns for coordination, balance, strength, endurance, and daily participation.

03

Participation

Practice is built around access, autonomy, and the movement skills that can support greater independence.

Athlete. Provider. Movement Partner.

Hi, I'm Johnny, though most people know me as Scrawny Johnny.

Since I was young, I've always been moving. I grew up on the soccer field, then transitioned to competitive swimming and water polo through high school and junior college. When that chapter closed, I didn't step away from activity. I doubled down, developing a deeper, more intentional relationship with strength, conditioning, and overall fitness inside and out of the gym.

During college, I worked inside a gym-based supplement shop, where training, nutrition, and community weren't just concepts. They were daily practice. That environment shaped how I approach health: not as idea, but as an every day experience.

Academically, I studied Kinesiology at Mt. San Antonio College and Speech-Language Pathology at Cal State San Marcos, bridging the gap combining both my passions of understanding physical performance and harboring communication and connection.

In 2020, I came under the mentorship of Dawnmarie Gaivin, founder of the Speller's Method. I spent nearly five years as a spelling provider at Spellers Center San Diego, supporting nonspeaking individuals in accessing reliable communication. While I am not currently offering spelling services, I continue to support spellers, families, and spelling providers through motor coaching that respects communication access, autonomy, and presumption of competence.

Today, I bring these disciplines together through Get Scrawny, where athletic structure is built to support your access to communication. My work focuses on motor planning, physical coaching, and strengthening systems that support both movement and expression.
